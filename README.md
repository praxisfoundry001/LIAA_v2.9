# Legislative Integrity and Accountability Act

## Open-Source Model Legislation for Preventing Congressional Financial Conflicts and Protecting Public Trust

**Current public version:** v2.9
**Current DOI:** https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22693996

The **Legislative Integrity and Accountability Act (LIAA)** is open-source model federal legislation designed to prevent congressional financial conflicts prospectively.

Rather than depending on proof of insider trading, corrupt motive, use of material nonpublic information, market effect, or a causal connection between a financial interest and a legislative act, LIAA separates covered federal legislative power and privileged governmental information from privately attributable, materially particularized financial exposure.

The proposal covers Members of Congress, senior congressional staff, spouses, and dependent children through person-specific attribution. It preserves ordinary diversified saving, retirement, broad market participation, and United States Treasury investment while regulating financial exposure capable of creating the conflict the Act addresses.

## What LIAA does

LIAA establishes an effective-economic-exposure boundary rather than relying solely on product labels or nominal ownership.

Its architecture includes:

* direct, indirect, beneficial, synthetic, and economically equivalent financial exposure;
* person-specific attribution rather than automatic household vicarious liability;
* look-through, anti-synthesis, concentration, derivative, digital-asset, municipal, and government-event-contract rules;
* structured transition, divestiture, extension, tolling, and successor-state accounting;
* a LIAA Transition Trust that separates control without erasing beneficial exposure or restarting compliance obligations;
* a conforming Certificate of Divestiture amendment to 26 U.S.C. § 1043;
* function-specific administration across House and Senate ethics offices, the Office of Government Ethics, the Clerk of the House, the Secretary of the Senate, GAO, DOJ, and Article III courts;
* a machine-readable Public Compliance Ledger with correction, privacy, accessibility, API, and lineage requirements;
* fitted civil remedies and person-specific liability;
* and periodic GAO review without automatic statutory expiration.

## What LIAA does not do

LIAA is not an insider-trading prosecution statute.

Compliance does not depend on proving that a covered person traded on privileged information, acted with corrupt motive, altered legislation because of a financial interest, or caused a market movement.

The Act does not create an additional constitutional qualification for congressional office, regulate legislative acts or legislative motive, or prohibit ordinary diversified saving and retirement investment.

## Why v2.9

Version 2.9 is a substantial revision of the prior published LIAA version.

The underlying public job survives: prospectively preventing financial conflicts created by the coexistence of congressional power or privileged governmental information and private financial exposure.

The architecture is materially stronger.

Among other changes, the revised Act:

* replaces a primarily issuer-specific trading boundary with an effective-economic-exposure framework;
* repairs the senior-staff boundary;
* separates public persons, household persons, and attribution vehicles;
* directly regulates spouse and dependent-child exposure without automatic Member liability;
* repairs the divestiture/trading collision through the Compliance Transaction;
* introduces structured successor-state and transition accounting;
* replaces the earlier trust endpoint with the LIAA Transition Trust;
* establishes function-specific institutional carriers;
* rebuilds disclosure around a public compliance-lineage architecture;
* fits remedies to distinct violation classes;
* and replaces automatic sunset architecture with standing law and periodic review.

## Status

LIAA v2.9 is **open-source model federal legislation**.

It is not enacted law.

It is not an introduced bill unless and until introduced by a Member of Congress.

It is not an official product of Congress, GAO, OGE, DOJ, the House Committee on Ethics, the Senate Select Committee on Ethics, or any other Federal institution.

It is intended as a technically reviewable legislative proposal. Ordinary technical conforming edits by congressional legislative counsel may be appropriate if the proposal is prepared for introduction, provided those edits do not alter its substantive design.
